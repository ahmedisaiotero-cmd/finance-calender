# SYNC Principles

This document defines the permanent product principles for Sync.

These principles should guide every future agent, Cursor task, Codex task, product decision, and implementation.

Sync exists to reduce mental load by helping people understand what matters in their life, when it matters.

---

## 1. Intelligence before interface

Build smarter understanding before adding more pages.

New surfaces should not compensate for weak intelligence. If Sync does not understand, rank, explain, or communicate well enough, improve the shared brain first.

---

## 2. Context over data

Sync is not just storing facts.

It helps the user understand what those facts mean together: what changed, what connects, what matters now, and what can stay quiet.

---

## 3. Calm over urgency

Sync should reduce pressure, not create it.

Urgency belongs only where there is real time sensitivity or risk. Most communication should feel steady, clear, and grounded.

---

## 4. Trust before automation

Sync should earn trust before asking for deeper integrations or acting on behalf of the user.

The user should be able to see what Sync remembered, what it understood, why something surfaced, and how to correct it.

---

## 5. Evidence before inference

Sync should only personalize or interpret based on actual evidence:

- memory
- consequence
- profile
- timing
- repeated pattern
- user-provided context

When confidence is low, Sync should say so.

---

## 6. Explain before advising

If Sync suggests something, it should be able to explain why.

Advice without explanation feels controlling. Explanation makes guidance trustworthy.

---

## 7. Respect user agency

Sync should never command, guilt, shame, or control the user.

Avoid language like "you need to" or "you should." Prefer calm guidance: "it may be worth," "the next thing worth protecting," or "this could make tomorrow lighter."

---

## 8. Silence is better than noise

If nothing useful needs to be said, Sync should stay quiet.

The absence of pressure is part of the product. A calm empty state is better than filler.

---

## 9. Specific over generic

Specific language builds trust.

Avoid vague phrases like "important items," "worth keeping in view," or "stay on track." Name the actual thing: rent, payday, workout, school, Mom's birthday, rough sleep, a quiet week.

---

## 10. Life is not just productivity

Sync is not a task manager.

It should understand worries, relationships, health, money, family, goals, ideas, routines, emotions, preferences, and quiet context — not only tasks and calendar events.

---

## 11. Integrations feed intelligence

Calendar, finance, health, email, and other integrations are inputs into the Sync intelligence system.

They should not become separate products, dashboards, or domain agents. They should help Sync understand life more clearly.

---

## 12. Continuity matters

Sync should not wake up with amnesia every day.

It should understand the story forming across days and weeks: what is becoming consistent, what is fading, what is getting heavier, and what has become calmer.

---

## 13. One brain, many surfaces

Today, Daily Brief, Pulse, Memory, My Life, and future notifications should share the same intelligence.

Do not fork ranking, meaning, consequence, or voice logic into page-specific systems when it belongs in the shared Sync brain.

---

## 14. Human-readable intelligence

Every important output should be understandable, explainable, and grounded.

Scores, categories, and internal metadata are only useful if they become clear human meaning.

---

## 15. Consequences over raw recall

Sync should not merely repeat what the user entered.

It should explain what follows: what changes today, what affects tomorrow, what connects to another commitment, and what matters because of the user's current life context.

---

## 16. Fewer, better signals

Sync should prefer one to three useful signals over a long list.

The goal is not completeness by default. The goal is clarity.

---

## 17. Categories are not agents

Money, Health, Family, Work, Relationships, and Personal are life areas.

They are not separate intelligence layers. Sync has one shared brain that understands many kinds of life context.

---

## 18. The product should feel lighter as it gets smarter

More intelligence should not create more clutter.

As Sync learns more, the experience should become calmer, more specific, and easier to trust.

---

## The permanent test

Before building, changing, or expanding Sync, ask:

1. Does this reduce mental load?
2. Does this improve shared intelligence?
3. Is it grounded in evidence?
4. Can Sync explain it?
5. Does it respect the user?
6. Is it calmer than the alternative?
7. Does it avoid turning Sync into a dashboard, planner, chatbot, or productivity system?

If the answer is no, do not build it.
